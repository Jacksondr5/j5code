# Crews

## Problem

Complex work needs a group of agents with defined jobs working together with a high degree of independence from the person — a builder, a reviewer, and a sitter on one pull request is the canonical case. Wiring such a group by hand every time is slow and error-prone, groups that die leave stranded obligations, and a Foreground agent that keeps the person's attention needs somewhere to hand context-heavy work so it can stay responsive ([problems](../problems.md): large unsupervised groups, human attention, more work needs more cleanup).

## Definition

A **Crew** is a group of agents launched as a unit from a **roster its Captain proposes and the person approves**, composed for the brief in front of it. Nobody writes a Crew file in advance. The person starts a Crew by sending `/crew <brief>` in any thread (web and desktop): the brief goes to that thread's agent, whatever saved agent, model, and policy it already runs as, wrapped in the platform's guidance to compose a Crew rather than do the work. No dedicated Captain agent exists; the agent that proposes becomes the Captain. It reads the saved-agent library and proposes a roster — a name, the brief every seat will start on, and one seat per agent with a one-line reason and, when useful, seat instructions. The proposal is a **human gate** answered where it was asked: inline above the Captain's composer, like a planning question, and never on the inbox bell (reaffirmed by Bryant, 2026-09-14). The person may drop seats, add agents from the library with a seat name and reason, then approve the final roster or decline. Approval spawns every seat as a persona-backed Peer Agent placed under the Captain, records the roster snapshot (the _Manifest_ in implementation vocabulary: name, brief, members, who approved each and why, version), and tells the Captain how the launch went once every seat has opened its first turn or failed to: one **launch report** naming what the person changed against the proposal and, per seat, started, failed with the run's error, or not yet started after a minute. Dispatching a brief commits intent, not a running seat, so nothing is said before that (Jackson's dogfood, 2026-09-17). A decline is told at once. Each seat's first turn carries the platform's identity facts, the crew context and roster, any seat instructions, its handoff obligation, and the brief.

**Membership changes only through the gate.** When the work needs a seat the roster lacks, the Captain asks for one agent with a reason and an optional brief for that seat. The request lands in the person's Inbox and counts on the bell, because the person may be anywhere mid-run; approval appends the seat to the snapshot and bumps its version, and either decision is posted to the Captain, who keeps working meanwhile. A Crew holds at most **twelve seats**, initial roster and additions together, counting requests still open. Crew members cannot propose or grow Crews — wanting more hands is an escalation to the Captain.

**Human approval is the authority.** The person who approves a seat decides what access it runs with, so approved seats run with their own agent's runtime policy even when the Captain is read-only; the gate shows each seat's agent, the access it will have, and any seat instructions before the person approves. Seats are checked where they are structured: an agent missing from the library or turned off, a duplicate seat name, or a roster past the cap is refused to the Captain with the next step, before anyone spawns. Prose in a brief is never parsed or validated.

**Handoffs are files, not chat.** A seat whose agent definition declares an output artifact (a Critic's ReviewHandoff, a Scout's ContextBrief) owes it as the same shared handoff file every saved agent writes — `handoffs/<agent>/<Artifact>-<task>.md` in the project's artifacts (see the [persona contract](../agent-personas/index.md)) — and its first turn names that exact path. The handoff gate checks for the file when a run ends and reminds the seat once. **Every seat tells its Captain when it finishes**, as measured facts: how the run ended first, the run's error when it failed, and whether its handoff was written, missing, or never declared, with the handoff inline when short. A first turn that fails is a launch outcome, told once by the launch report and not again as a finish; and a seat's failure that its Captain hears this way is not repeated by the silence detector as an unanswered ask. The notice is posted the first time a seat finishes and again only when those facts change; a finish that would repeat the last notice word for word is silent, because the seat's own reply already reached the Captain. Notices that arrive while the Captain's turn is running fold into the one seat notice already queued behind that turn, so the Captain absorbs its Crew's news in one turn rather than one per seat. A Captain never has to interview its Crew to learn a seat is done, and it never waits for one inside a turn: `t3_thread_wait` is not on its surface, because a turn that blocks cannot receive the notice it is waiting for. The person opens every handoff on the Artifacts page. A message in chat is never a handoff.

**Members report their finish.** When a member's run ends and it owes no open reply, the platform tells its Captain how the run ended and where the handoff stands; a later message runs normally. The platform settles no seat: upstream settles by hand, by merged PR, or by idle days, and a seat marked settled seconds after launch read as wrong (Jackson, 2026-09-17). **A Crew's state is its seats' states**, summarized from measured facts — how many seats are running, need a person, or were settled — on the Crew's row of the Fleet page and on the Captain's expander in the sidebar; the person never has to interview a Crew to learn where it stands. Members stay out of the thread sidebar unless pinned; the Captain's row carries the Captain's anchor mark and an expander into its members; the Fleet page lists every agent by Squadron and placement with each Crew under its Captain. Crews are a web and desktop surface; mobile has no Crew surface today, deliberately.

### Captains

Any agent with Crews placed under it is a **Captain** — a derived, measured status, not a platform entity or a tier. The management layer is made of agents in the placement tree, which nests to any depth; there is no container above the Crew.

- **You command what you brief.** The Captain that proposed and briefed a Crew commands it. Proposing a Crew _for_ someone else is proxy management; instead, send the decision and the brief to the future Captain.
- **One Captain, many Crews.** Concurrent work gets concurrent Crews: a Captain may propose another Crew whenever the work splits, each through its own gate and named for what it is for, and every later request, stop, and archive names the Crew it means. The Captain's expander in the sidebar and its node on the Fleet page show one named, collapsible group per Crew.
- **Agents run Crews.** Captains coordinating Crews without the person in the loop is the point; the person's part is the gate. A member's attempt to grow or archive a Crew, or to spawn a Peer Agent of its own, is refused with an error naming escalation to its Captain; a member that needs a helper runs a provider-native Subagent inside its own session (Bryant, 2026-09-14, closing the question left open on 2026-08-24).
- **A Captain may stop the Crews directly under it.** `stop_crew` interrupts every seat with a running turn and leaves idle seats alone; nothing settles, nothing is retired, and every seat can be messaged again. The person has the same act as a Stop crew control on the Crew's Fleet header and on the Captain's sidebar expander, offered only while a seat is running.
- **A Captain may archive the Crews directly under it** — the only agent-invoked archive, and it triggers the same loud machinery as any archive. Not grandchild Crews, not individual members, not solo agents. A Captain that still commands a live Crew is never archived alone: an agent's `archive_agent` on it is refused naming the Crew to retire first, and when a person archives or deletes a Captain thread from any surface, the platform retires the Crews it commanded as units the moment the archive commits, the web dialog having shown every live Crew seat by seat beforehand. The person has the same Archive crew act on the Crew's Fleet header.
- **Captains are never routers.** Any participant may message any participant; the hierarchy carries decisions, never messages.
- **A Captain's runtime policy does not gate the platform's verbs.** Stopping, archiving, and retiring what it commands are decided by identity (only the Captain, only its own Crew) and by the human's confirmation, never by the Captain's sandbox: a read-only Captain runs its Crew's lifecycle like any other (Bryant, 2026-09-14). The sandbox guards the workspace; the platform guards its own verbs.

### Lifecycle

Crews are deliberately disposable. They **archive only as a unit** — members are never individually archived or replaced — because an agent's death is usually recoverable by messaging it again, and an irrecoverably poisoned member has likely contaminated its crewmates. Recovery is **propose again**, which is cheap because a Background Crew's value lives in its brief, its approved roster, and its durable artifacts — worktree, branch, pull request, handoffs — which survive the agents. Two platform rules make this safe: archive never destroys work, and archive is loud, with every open Exchange ending in a notice to its waiter.

**Archive and respawn are judgment moments, and the platform never makes that decision itself.** Archiving a Crew with open Exchanges or running turns refuses with the list, seat by seat, and requires a confirmation token; the expectation, carried in guidance rather than enforced, is that a Captain checks with the person and they plan together. The successor Crew starts from a fresh proposal and a fresh brief written by whoever respawns it — where things stand, the state of the durable artifacts, what to do next. There is no auto-forwarded original brief, no generated summary, no transferred conversation, no inherited obligations, no auto-reopened asks; the archived Crew's brief, roster, ledger, and handoffs stay readable for the respawner to consult.

The platform ships the machinery — propose, gate, spawn, render, remind, settle, archive. The Captain agent that composes a Crew, the Roles it seats, and any Playbook it follows are always the user's content: the PR Group is one Captain's proposal someone approved, never the product's opinion.

## Acceptance criteria

### Launch and the gate

1. `/crew <brief>` in any web or desktop thread sends the brief, wrapped in the platform's crew-composition guidance, as that thread's next turn; the thread keeps its own agent, model, and policy, no saved agent is required, and a command without a brief is refused before anything is sent.
2. A roster proposal renders inline above the Captain's composer; the person can remove seats, add agents from the library with a seat name and reason, then approve the final roster or decline.
3. The gate shows, for every seat, the agent, the access it will run with, and any seat instructions, before the person approves.
4. A seat naming an agent missing from or disabled in the library, a duplicate seat name, or a roster past twelve seats is refused to the Captain with the next step, before anything spawns; prose is never parsed.
5. Filing a proposal or a seat request is itself the human gate and works under every sandbox and approval policy, including Codex approval policy `never`.

### The unit

6. Approval spawns every seat as a Peer Agent placed under the Captain and records the roster snapshot with each member's approver and reason; a second approval of the same proposal spawns nothing, and a decision that races another device's on the same gate is refused rather than undoing it. The Captain is told by one launch report, posted once every seat has produced provider activity, completed, or failed (or a minute has passed without confirmation), naming what the person changed against the proposal and each seat's outcome with the run's error; a seat whose provider is signed out is refused at spawn with that reason, before anything is created.
7. A spawn that fails hands the gate back to the person; retrying converges on the same seats rather than refusing them as taken. A decision the server lost mid-way is settled at the next boot: a decline is finished, an approval is handed back.
8. A Crew lives in exactly one Squadron and renders as one node under its Captain with its members beneath it.
9. Every member can message any participant in the Squadron; the Crew's contact with the person goes through the inbox or its Captain rather than chat — a plain send to the person is refused by the [human-ask rule](../a2a/agent-tools.md), so a seat reaches the person only with an ask that lands in the Inbox or a reply on an open Exchange.

### Growing

10. A Captain adds a seat only through a request the person answers in the Inbox, where it also counts on the bell; approval appends the seat and bumps the snapshot version; either decision is posted to the Captain.
11. A Crew holds at most twelve seats, counting open requests; a member's request is refused naming escalation to its Captain.

### Handoffs

12. A seat whose definition declares an output artifact writes it as its handoff file in the project's artifacts; its first turn names the exact path.
13. When a seat finishes, the Captain receives a notice that leads with how the run ended and, for a failed run, carries the run's recorded error, then whether the seat's handoff was written, missing, or never declared, a written handoff riding inline when short and by path otherwise; a finish whose notice would repeat the seat's last one is silent, and a notice arriving while the Captain's turn runs folds into the seat notice already queued behind it rather than queuing another turn.
14. A seat that finishes without its handoff is reminded once and is reported to its Captain on its next finished run whatever happened.

### Captains

15. A Captain shows the anchor mark, derived from placement and nothing else, with the Crews it commands in its tooltip; each Crew it commands renders as its own named, collapsible group with that Crew's state summary, and solo agents it spawned sit in a separate group; members stay out of the sidebar unless pinned.
16. A Captain may archive a Crew placed directly under it and nothing else; a member's attempt to grow or archive a Crew, or to spawn a Peer Agent, is refused naming escalation to its Captain. Members may still run provider-native Subagents.
17. A Captain that commands a live Crew is never archived alone: an agent's request is refused naming the Crew to retire first; a person's archive or deletion of the Captain's thread, from any surface, retires the Crews it commanded as units when it commits, and the web dialog lists each live Crew's seats with their running turns and open asks before the person confirms. The person can also retire one Crew from its Fleet header behind the same seat-by-seat dialog, and a seat can never be archived on its own from the app.

### Lifecycle

18. A member whose run ends owing no reply is reported to its Captain; the platform settles neither members nor Captains, and upstream's settle rules apply to both.
19. A Crew archives only as a unit; archiving never deletes a worktree, branch, or checkpoint; archiving with open Exchanges or running turns lists them seat by seat and requires a confirmation token.
20. A successor Crew starts from a new proposal and a fresh brief; nothing from the archived Crew's conversation, obligations, or asks is carried over automatically, and its brief, roster, ledger, and handoffs stay readable: the Fleet page lists a Squadron's retired Crews with the brief, the roster snapshot (seat, agent, who approved it and why, version), and a way to the Captain's thread; handoffs stay on the Artifacts page.
21. A Crew stops as a unit: its Captain over `stop_crew`, or the person from the Crew's Fleet header or the Captain's expander, interrupts every seat with a running turn; idle seats are untouched, nothing settles or is retired, and the result names each seat as interrupted, already idle, or archived.

### State

22. A Crew's row on the Fleet page and its Captain's expander in the sidebar summarize the seats' states from measured facts (running, failed, needing a person, settled, unknown); a seat whose last run failed reads as failed rather than idle; idle seats are the unspoken default, and no state is inferred from silence.

## Scenarios

- **A pull-request Crew.** In Billing Migration the user sends `/crew Land the invoice-export PR: build, review, sit on CI.` The Captain proposes Builder, Critic, and Sitter with a reason each; the user drops the Sitter, approves, and the two seats appear under the Captain with the brief and the roster. The Critic finishes and writes its ReviewHandoff into the project's artifacts; the Captain's thread receives the notice with the findings inline. (AC1, AC2, AC6, AC8, AC12, AC13)
- **Two Crews at once.** In Website Redesign the user, mid-conversation with the agent that has been planning the launch, sends `/crew Migrate the blog to the new CMS` and then `/crew Rewrite the pricing page copy`. The same thread proposes two rosters, each answered above its composer; both appear under the Captain's row as named groups, Blog Migration and Pricing Copy, each with its own toggle, state, and Stop. (AC1, AC2, AC15)
- **More hands.** The Captain judges the CI failures need a Sitter and requests one; the request appears in the Inbox and on the bell, the user approves, and the seat joins the roster at version 2. The Builder tries to request a helper itself and is refused, naming escalation to the Captain. (AC10, AC11)
- **A flaky launch.** The provider is unavailable when the user approves an added seat. The gate reopens; the user approves again and the seat spawns onto its reserved place instead of being refused as taken. (AC7)
- **Stop everything.** The Builder is heading down a path the person recognizes as wrong while the Critic is mid-review. The person clicks Stop crew on the Crew's Fleet header; both running turns end, the seats stay on the roster, and the Captain briefs them again. (AC21)
- **A poisoned Crew.** The Critic has gone wrong and confused its crewmates. The Captain asks to archive the Crew; the refusal lists two open asks and one running turn seat by seat with a token; the Captain confirms after checking with the user, writes a fresh brief from the surviving branch and handoffs, and proposes a successor. (AC16, AC19, AC20)
