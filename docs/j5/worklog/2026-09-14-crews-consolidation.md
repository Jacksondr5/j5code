# Crews: from a definition file to a proposed roster, and one artifact path

## 2026-09-09 — composed at launch, not read from a file

Jackson and Bryant settled that a Crew is composed at launch: the person sends `/crew <brief>` on a fresh thread, the thread launches as the `crew-captain` saved agent, and the Captain reads the library with `list_agents` and files a roster with `propose_crew`. The person approves, edits, or declines; approval spawns the seats under the Captain and records the roster snapshot. The Captain grows a Crew only through `request_crew_member`, which goes through the same gate. The earlier definition-file, Crews-tab, and `@crew` slice was reverted before shipping. Twelve seats is the hard cap.

## 2026-09-10 — where the gate lives, who decides access, what a seat owes

Bryant moved the initial roster gate inline above the Captain's composer, like a planning question ("the launch confirmation belongs where the user asked, not on the bell"); mid-run seat requests stay on the inbox bell because the person may be anywhere. Bryant ruled that **human approval is the authority**: an approved seat runs with its own agent's runtime policy, so a read-only Captain commands writing seats once a person approved them, and there is no auto-approval because runbooks are not built. Codex 0.153 rejected the gate verbs outright under approval policy `never`; they were pre-approved for that policy only, and their descriptions say the call is itself the human gate. The same day Bryant asked that a seat's declared output artifact reach the Captain as a record, not prose; the first cut was a crew-only `deliver_artifact` verb with a ledger table.

## 2026-09-14 — review findings, and one artifact path

A Critic and Sentry review of `temp/crews` produced nine Medium findings. Landed on the branch the same day: Codex pre-approval narrowed from a server-wide default to a per-tool list; the roster card shows each seat's instructions and access; a retried approval recognizes the member rows its failed spawn reserved; `archive_agent` refuses a Captain with a live Crew; the HTTP seat schema carries the MCP bounds; agent-authored text cannot close platform tags in the Captain's notice.

Bryant first asked that delivered artifacts appear on the Artifacts page rather than as Fleet rows, with versions segmented inside one file. Then, once `feature/agent-handoff-artifacts` (PR #142) shipped the general mechanism — every saved agent with a declared output writes `handoffs/<agent>/<Artifact>-<task>.md` itself, a run-finalization observer checks for it, nudges once, and marks it missing — he chose to **consolidate on that single path with the smallest blast radius from `j5/main`**. `temp/crews` was rebased onto that branch (squashed; the granular history is on `temp/crews-pre-rebase-5`), the crew-only verb, ledger table, migration, and Fleet rows were removed, and the crew settler gained the one Crew-specific piece: when a seat's handoff file exists at the end of a run, the Captain is told in its thread, keyed by the file's modification time. `write_artifact` joined the read-only pre-approval lists for Codex and Claude because a handoff lives in application storage, never in the sandboxed workspace. Version segmentation inside one file was **deferred**: it would belong in the shared writer, not in Crews.

The rebase also brought the stack's multi-environment J5 client. Crew proposals became a per-environment read source shared by the bell, the Inbox page, and the inline gate, which closed the reviewers' multi-environment and duplicate-polling findings for the gate. The PR #109 artifact findings were addressed on the branch: the change subscription is a J5 RPC group, listing sorts before it truncates, the Artifacts page re-reads a preview only when its own file changed, not-found responses come from a typed reason, and `FORK.md` case 33 describes what exists.

## Decided after the review (Bryant, 2026-09-14)

- **Gate placement stands.** The initial roster is answered inline in the Captain's thread and never on the bell; a Captain's request to add a member goes to the bell and is never inline. No extra indicator elsewhere (Critic Q5 closed).
- **Read-only personas may run lifecycle verbs.** `stop_agent`, `archive_agent`, and `archive_crew` are gated by identity and the human's confirmation, not by the caller's sandbox (Sentry S-4 closed).

## Completion work landed the same day

After the consolidation, the partially complete items were finished on `temp/crews`: the Fleet page, Crew chips, and the member expander read every connected environment through the same per-environment client the gate uses (one keyed batch store now serves thread homes, memberships, and spawned children); every seat settlement posts a `<j5_seat_settled>` notice to its Captain with the run status and the handoff as written, missing, or never declared; a Crew's row on the Fleet page and the Captain's expander summarize seat states from measured facts; and a `crew-captain` definition ships as the twelfth bundled example so `/crew` works on a fresh install.

## Verified live (2026-09-14, evening)

With Bryant's go-ahead, the whole flow ran on his dev server through the controlled browser: `/crew` launched a Codex Captain (gpt-5.6-sol, approval policy `never`); `list_agents` and `propose_crew` succeeded without an approval prompt, proving the per-tool Codex pre-approval; the roster gate rendered inline with each seat's agent, access, and instructions; approval spawned a Codex Scout and a Claude Critic, the `<j5_crew_gate>` notice landed, the sidebar showed the Captain chip and "2 crew · 2 running", and the Fleet page showed the Crew under its Captain with the same summary. Both seats wrote their handoffs with `write_artifact` (Codex under `never`, Claude in `dontAsk`) with no permission denial, the files appeared on the Artifacts page, and each settlement posted a `<j5_seat_settled>` notice with `handoff: written`. A steered request produced a `request_crew_member` that counted on the bell and rendered in the Inbox's Crew requests; approving it added a Claude Sentry at roster version 2, which also wrote its handoff and settled. `archive_crew` refused with a token and completed on the retry; the Crew, its three seats, the Fleet group, and the chip all closed out.

Found and fixed the same evening: the handoff file name took the first eight characters of the thread id, which for deterministic crew-seat ids is always `thread:j` (a colon in the name and one shared file per persona and kind across every Crew); non-uuid ids now take eight hex characters of a hash. Found and recorded: the Captain interpreted "wait for the human's decision" as a `wait` tool call and kept its turn running, so every notice queued behind it (the queue-vs-steer ruling makes platform notices queue); the bundled Captain and the documented YAML now say to end the turn and never wait inside one. Both of the follow-ups this surfaced (one shared log file per Crew, `?` origin for a person's Captain) were fixed the same evening; see below.

## Decided in the closing questions (Bryant, 2026-09-14, evening)

- **Build `stop_crew`.** Captain-only over MCP, plus a Stop crew control for the person on the Fleet crew header and the Captain's sidebar expander through a J5 HTTP route. It interrupts every seat with a running turn, leaves idle seats alone, settles and archives nothing, and reports each seat as interrupted or already idle.
- **Members never spawn Peer Agents.** `spawn_agent` refuses a Crew member with escalation to its Captain; members keep provider-native Subagents through `delegate_task`. Closes the 2026-08-24 open question.
- **Small items landed:** the Crew clause of Roles AC16 retired; a person's own launch shows `human` on the Fleet page (an agent with a Squadron home and no placement row is one no agent spawned, since every agent path records placement at creation); each Crew seat gets its own provider log file (a hash tail on truncated segments).
- **Deferred:** handoff versioning inside one file goes to PR #142 as a change to the shared writer; Playbooks, Memos, and the successor Crew view wait for dogfooding; mobile stays without a Crew surface.
- **Process:** this work stays local. After the last two stack PRs merge, `temp/crews` is rebased onto `j5/main` and cut into PRs from there.

## Built after the closing questions

`stop_crew` landed as decided: a `CrewStopService` reads the Crew, checks the caller (the Captain over MCP; nobody for the person, who is not a participant), and interrupts every seat with a running turn through the ordinary thread interrupt, reporting each seat as `interrupt_requested`, `already_idle`, or `archived`. The MCP verb sits beside `archive_crew` with the same Captain-only and squadron checks and is pre-approved for Codex under `never` and allowed for Claude in `dontAsk`. The person reaches the same service through a J5 HTTP route under the operate scope; the web shows a Stop crew button on the Crew's Fleet header and a Stop control beside the Captain's sidebar expander, both only while a seat is running, both keyed so a double click cannot mint two command sets. The `crew-captain` example and the documented YAML tell the Captain when to reach for it.

Found on Bryant's dev Fleet the same night: retired agents never left the page. Since archive became reversible (PR #132) an archived participant keeps its membership row with an `archived_at` stamp, and the Fleet read listed every membership without looking at the stamp. The read now drops archived agents and keeps one only as a dimmed placeholder above an active descendant, as the Fleet definition's AC11 always said. The other leftovers were sixteen seat threads whose Captains had been archived from the sidebar: archive never cascades to spawned children, and those seats' Crew records had been lost when the crews migration was renumbered, so they sat at the Squadron root as plain agents. They were archived through the app's own `thread.archive` command, which the lifecycle bridge mirrored into the ledger.

Later the same night a Captain on Bryant's dev server sat with ten queued turns. It had called upstream's `t3_thread_wait` on a seat and blocked for fifteen minutes; under the queue-versus-steer ruling every seat notice queued behind that turn, so the Captain was starving itself of the news it was waiting for, and each of its three seats had finished two or three runs, each run its own notice, each notice its own future turn. Three changes, all decided by Bryant: `t3_thread_wait` leaves the J5 surface; a seat notice folds into the seat notice already queued behind a running Captain turn (upstream's `queued-run.edit`) so the Captain reads a digest in one turn; and a notice is posted on first finish and on change only, since a run whose notice would repeat the last one word for word was a reply the Captain already received. The bundled Captain's wording about ending its turn was carried into Bryant's imported definition as well, and its stray `outputArtifact` removed.

## The person's side of the lifecycle (2026-09-15)

A review of the definition against the branch found three criteria only half built. AC17 refused a Captain's archive only over MCP: the person's sidebar archive of a Captain went through untouched, which is how sixteen seats had been stranded the night before. AC20 kept a retired Crew's snapshot in the database with nothing on the web reading it. AC9 turned out to be enforced already — a plain send to the person is refused by the human-ask rule, so a seat reaches the person only through an Inbox ask — and only the definition needed to say so. Built for the other two, all on the J5 side: the pre-archive read attaches the live Crews the agent commands with each seat's running turn and open asks; the archive dialog lists them, and a lifecycle cascade on the crew event stream retires each Crew as a unit when the Captain's archive or deletion commits, so a mobile swipe or any future door meets the same rule as the sidebar (the first cut ran the cascade in the web delegate; a review the same day moved it server-side). The dialog also refuses to archive a seat on its own, as `archive_agent` does. The unit archive service accepts a person as caller (no Captain check, no token: the dialog was the confirmation), which also gave the Fleet crew header an Archive crew control behind the same seat-by-seat dialog. The Fleet read now carries each Crew's brief, version, and roster snapshot with approver and reason, and the page lists a Squadron's retired Crews collapsed beneath its agents with a way to the Captain's thread.

## The pre-push review (2026-09-15)

Before cutting PRs, the review comments on the merged agents stack were read for their themes (comments that do not match runtime behavior, FORK.md discipline, unrequested scope, per-row polling, weak tests) and the branch was reviewed against them. Decided by Bryant and built the same day: `delegate_task` and its status and cancel verbs join the pre-approval lists, because refused members were being sent to a verb the sandbox then rejected; the Captain cascade moved from the web delegate into a server-side reaction on the crew event stream so mobile and any future door meet the same rule; the speculative runbook axis (`approvedBy: runbook`, `auto_approved`, `runbook_declared`) was cut, since nothing wrote it and the definition says there is no auto-approval; the PR #109 artifact refactor will be cut as its own PR, with its dropped listing guard and trace attribute restored; and the Fleet header keeps Archive crew as the person's half of AC17. Bugs fixed with tests: a stopped seat no longer settles or wakes its Captain (interrupted is not finished), the settler daemon resumes from its last event, a retired Crew's seat is a plain agent again, a seat cannot be archived alone from the app, a missing Crew read warns instead of reading as no Crews, the Fleet tree descends into a seat's own children, the expander drops retired children, a plain approvals-on thread cannot spawn a write-capable saved agent, and a declined request releases the seats a failed spawn reserved. Performance: rows without children no longer subscribe to the shell list, Crew reads are incremental and batched under the route cap, and the always-on Fleet badge no longer carries retired rosters. FORK.md corrections: the `t3_thread_wait` re-declaration sentence, the case numbering (Issue #105 stays 34; the provider-log case is 36, after #144 took 35), the missing ledger rows, and the RPC merge count.
